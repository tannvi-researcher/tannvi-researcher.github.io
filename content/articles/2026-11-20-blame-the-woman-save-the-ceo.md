title: "Blame the Woman, Save the CEO"
date: 2026-11-20

Two people had an affair. One of them ran the most valuable chip company on earth. The other ran a data centre business in Singapore. Guess which one the world decided was the weapon.

Within days of the story breaking, [Alice Huang](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261109-who-is-alice-huang-chinese-exec-accused-of-spying-on-nvidia-ceo) stopped being a person and became a plot device. Headlines called her a spy, a Chinese asset, a honey trap. Columnists wrote about her as if she had slipped into Jensen Huang's life through a vent in the ceiling. Nobody had charged her with anything. Singapore had looked at her company and [found too little to prosecute](https://mothership.sg/2026/11/alice-huang-jensen-huang-who/). The Justice Department [says it has reached no conclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal). Intelligence officials themselves [are arguing over whether the spy label holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal). None of that slowed anyone down. A leaked memo and a few anonymous sources were enough to turn her into a femme fatale.

Now look at him. Jensen Huang was the CEO. He chose the relationship, chose the secrecy, and, [by his own admission](https://thehill.com/opinion/technology/6226189-nvidia-ceo-resigns-affair-spy/), chose to be unfaithful. He then [kept quiet while his company did business with hers](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/jensen-huangs-right-hand-man-oversaw-chip-customers-including-alice-huang). Those were his decisions, made with every resource and every adviser a man in his position could want. Yet the coverage treats him as the man things happened to. He was "targeted". He was "compromised". He issued an apology to his staff, [resigned](https://thehill.com/opinion/technology/6226189-nvidia-ceo-resigns-affair-spy/) with his fortune intact, and within a week the Financial Times was [reporting that he is weighing a return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal).

This is the oldest story we tell about power. When a powerful man falls, we go looking for the woman who pushed him. She is cunning; he is careless. She is a threat to the nation; he is a genius who had a lapse. Her reputation is spent so that his can be saved. One gets a scandal with an ending. The other gets a label with no expiry date.

If Alice Huang broke the law, charge her and prove it, in a court where she can answer back. That is what due process is for. What we are doing instead is older and uglier. We are letting suspicion do the work of evidence because she fits the shape of a villain we already know.

The real questions are about him and the system around him. How did Nvidia's chips keep reaching places they were not meant to go? Who knew? Who looked away? Those questions are harder, and they implicate powerful people. So it is easier to talk about her. Even Beijing [calls it a witch hunt](https://www.wionews.com/world/from-chip-war-to-political-witch-hunt-how-chinese-media-sees-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-s-affair-scandal-1794228462429), for its own reasons, which tells you how convenient the story has become for everyone except her.

Seduction makes a better headline than oversight. It always has.
