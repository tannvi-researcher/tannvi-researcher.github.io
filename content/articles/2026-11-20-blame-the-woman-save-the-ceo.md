Simone de Beauvoir said in her book, *The Second Sex*, that one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman.

[Alice Huang](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261109-who-is-alice-huang-chinese-exec-accused-of-spying-on-nvidia-ceo) runs a data centre business in Singapore. She is a Chinese national and former CEO of Megaspeed. And overnight, an anonymous document and some "unknown sources" have made her the witch that broke down the American economy: a spy, an asset, a honey trap, an object.

We glorified, sympathised with and victimised a 63-year-old CEO of the most valuable chip company on earth, who was cheating on his wife with [another woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal) apart from Alice Huang, reportedly. The man has [admitted to the affair](https://thehill.com/opinion/technology/6226189-nvidia-ceo-resigns-affair-spy/) and apologised to staff for his infidelity, while the same staff and the company [kept doing business with hers](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/jensen-huangs-right-hand-man-oversaw-chip-customers-including-alice-huang). They called the dealings normal, and only confirmed the affair when Nvidia's own general counsel asked. Even after The New York Times [reported a second affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal), for some reason, Alice Huang is the villain in the story of the AI bubble and the American economy.

With barely any facts, an anonymous source, unconfirmed evidence and no registered case, Alice Huang is social media's witch. On the contrary, the cheating man [resigned](https://thehill.com/opinion/technology/6226189-nvidia-ceo-resigns-affair-spy/) with his fortune completely intact and, within a couple of days, has already started [weighing a comeback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal). Another rich man, Elon Musk, it seems, said hiring him [would be an honor](https://fortune.com/2026/11/13/jensen-huang-nvidia-elon-musk-hire-honor/) (on X). The man was somehow targeted and compromised, and he was not, for some reason, cheating.

So the real question here is: what are we even trying to claim against the so-called cunning woman, the temptress, the Cleopatra, the b\*\*\*h, who has somehow caused the most brilliant man a lapse in judgment, a carelessness? He gets a scandal with an ending and, it seems, a sequel. She gets a label. No chance to answer back, no expiry date, just a stamp.

Singapore [investigated Megaspeed](https://mothership.sg/2026/11/mha-alice-huang-megaspeed/) over false declarations about server purchases and [found too little evidence to charge her](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/singapore-says-warns-alice-huang-lacks-evidence-of-offenses). It did find that she downplayed her role in the company. She got a stern warning and was [barred from re-entry](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/singapore-investigations-alice-huang-megaspeed-mha). Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs [says she was never investigated for spying](https://mothership.sg/2026/11/mha-alice-huang-megaspeed/). The spy claim rests on a leaked US memo and anonymous officials, officials who can't even say [when she started doing all of this, whether it was willingly at all, and what she has passed on](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/12/politics/alice-huang-us-intel-jensen-huang-nvidia). CNN's sources say there is no proof that she knowingly passed anything. Some officials think the label is overstated, and Representative Jim Himes [left a classified briefing doubtful](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/jim-himes-connecticut-democrat-face-the-nation-transcript-11-15-2026/). The Justice Department in the US has [reached no conclusion](https://qz.com/blanche-justice-department-no-conclusion-alice-huang-spy-111226), and she has not been charged with anything, anywhere. But somehow she has caused the most brilliant man to be targeted. Beijing itself has [called this a witch hunt](https://www.wionews.com/world/from-chip-war-to-political-witch-hunt-how-chinese-media-sees-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-s-affair-scandal-1794228462429), and of course for its own reasons. But then, what exactly are we even putting her on trial for?

Kate Manne, in *Down Girl*, uses the word "himpathy": the sympathy that powerful men get when they have done something wrong, while the woman involved gets suspicion or blame. Jensen is the compromised; Alice is the threat. Man by default is treated as the subject and woman as the object, as Simone said in *The Second Sex*. She has no voice in her own story. She just exists for something to happen to Jensen.

The femme fatale is a figure of male anxiety: a dangerous woman explaining a man's downfall so that he doesn't have to explain it himself. Somehow, she was the honey that trapped, but he was not a cheating man.

She isn't only a woman; she's a Chinese woman. She has her nationality and her gender attached to her. She's a seductive foreign agent, and the memes about the other Chinese wives and mistresses of other CEOs were something the internet had not held back on. She's the Orientalism of Western thought: the mysterious, deceptive Eastern dragon lady of the Western media stereotype. She fits into it perfectly.

Calling him compromised takes away his agency, which conveniently also takes away his responsibility. So perhaps cheating is not wrong, and it's completely fine to cheat on your wife. There are memes around the internet about how his wife is now letting him off. There are also memes about how all of manhood is begging Jensen Huang's wife not to divorce him. He is untouched by this whole story. He has been allowed to step out of his own bad choices.

So the media judges. It's a public trial. A label sticks long before any evidence is tested, any appeal is asked for, or any answers are allowed. That's the presumption of guilt in a trial by media. And the trump card in all of this is national security: in its name, ordinary standards of proof and fairness have all fallen apart. What we trust is whatever seems more convenient.

The monopoly of Nvidia, its domination of the AI market and its absolute reliance on one man, Jensen Huang, meant that his private life [wiped out around $1.5 trillion in market value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal). He is a national security asset in himself. Or perhaps this whole idea was to bring him down, but at the cost of a woman. Perhaps, in Tim Wu's words, it's the curse of bigness. Or, as Lina Khan has said, antitrust watches the prices and misses the power. We are forgetting that this man held the power in the system, and the whole of antitrust law is forgetting how much the market is anchored on one man and his personal life. The current market makes that plain. And perhaps when an anchor isn't reliable, the system removes the anchor itself, but at the cost of the woman in the situation.

It's the myth of the great man and the key person in the ecosystem: the great man who built the cult, the founder genius, who is somehow indispensable and blameless at the same time. It ties neatly to the idea of the prestige attached to a man's credit: his work in one space, his evil in others. We give this man a free pass because he's rich.

The board was meant to watch the CEO for the shareholders. The CEO ran a relationship with a customer's chair, and with a subordinate, and the board apparently did not even know. This is a governance failure. It is a failure in which a sexy headline mattered more than a so-called spy's life. And who pays for all of this? The people who hold shares in this company, and the modesty, personal dignity and respect of a woman called Alice Huang.

I don't know if seduction makes a better headline than oversight, or if saying one of the richest men in the world has been honey-trapped is easier and more convenient than saying he was careless and cheating. This is himpathy: a comeback story for a man who has already written his own story, while a woman is written out of hers. And that's what a monopoly does to an ecosystem. A man is too big to fail, and a woman is small enough to be blamed. She *reportedly* did something.

So, as [Chinua Achebe](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2013/03/22/175046327/chinua-achebe-and-the-bravery-of-lions), borrowing an African proverb, said: until the lions have their own historians, the history of the hunt will always glorify the hunter. Jensen Huang is the hunter.
