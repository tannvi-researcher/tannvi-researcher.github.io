"in the end, what you remember most are the things you didn't do," says ivy. "i don't know when my clock is going to run out. but i can tell you exactly what i will remember in those last 60 seconds."

ivy is giving a lecture to the other women in the old age home.

"what do you think you will remember in your last 60 seconds?" asks ivy.

an old lady says, "i'll remember the day my son passed away, and i had to live knowing that what i created doesn't exist anymore."

another woman says, "the moment i'll have on loop in my last 60 seconds is the day my mother asked me to leave my husband because he was hitting me. i remember her exact words. she said, love never looks like that."

another woman asks, "ivy, what will you remember the most?"

ivy says out loud, "hey, i am the teacher. i am supposed to be asking the questions."

the class got over. everybody was giggling. ivy packed up her bag: just a tiffin box with some orange seeds, a book, her iphone and her wired earphones. her daughter had come to pick her up. she was volunteering as a counsellor at the old age home. not to forget, ivy herself was fairly old. it's just that she was a lot fitter, at least physically, than the other people in the home.

on the car ride back, ivy's daughter asks her, "so, how was it today? i don't understand why you still have to do all of this. you can just sit at home. i'm here for you, mom."

ivy looks at her and says, "i don't know. maybe this is what keeps me alive. knowing that i am still of use."

ivy's daughter just looks at her weirdly and continues driving. a while later she says, "you're very quiet today, mom. what's up? what was the class about? tell me about your day. what was your question of the day today?"

ivy always asked a question at the end of a session. something existential, absurd, weird or philosophical. just to leave behind some open thoughts, to keep the other women in the old age home wondering, thinking, to give them something interesting.

"i asked them what they will remember in the last 60 seconds of their life."

ivy's daughter says, "i assume you'll have a flashback of your whole life, childhood to old age, in 60 seconds. all the good memories, all the happy moments, picked out and put together like a short movie. that would be very satisfying, wouldn't it, mom?"

"i don't think i will have a flashback of my happy moments," ivy says. "i think i'll remember the exact moment when i held his hand for the last time."

"whose hand? grandad's?"

"no. there was a boy. i was 23 when i met him.

i remember the first day i met him. i hated coffee, you know? and he had asked me to come to one of those very artisanal cafes, the ones that are so particular about the kind of roast you want and stuff like that. i didn't care for any of that. i already knew this was not somebody i would particularly enjoy. i don't like people who are too specific about their things. i know, i know, you'll say the amount of honey i want in my lemon tea in the morning says otherwise. but that 23-year-old girl isn't me anymore. that girl was just crazy. she had such high expectations from life.

i remember i entered the cafe. i was wearing a pink shirt. he was wearing a checked white shirt with brown pants. i looked at him, we caught eyes, he stood up. i was half an hour late. i remember i was wearing pjs, but with heels. i walked over to him and realised that even though i was wearing four-inch heels, he was fairly tall. that was a great tick on my checklist.

it was 6:30 on a thursday evening, the 16th of august, to be precise, when i met him. we talked till 12. in between, i remember i got up twice. once to call my mom and lie to her that i was at home. and the second time because my senior had called, asking if i'd found anything in my research. i wasn't researching. i was with a boy. i was supposed to be researching, but i really liked that boy, and i didn't really care about the research anymore. i had just started working at that point, and i wasn't the biggest fan of the work i was doing, so i didn't care for it much.

i remember we spoke about our jobs, our aspirations, growing up, childhood, parents, my sister. we spoke about marriage and finance. we were the last people in the cafe, and then the guy asked us to leave. he had already paid while i was in the washroom. i didn't get dinner that night. it was just some coffee and a piece of cake. he had made friends with the barista, even though it was his first time in that cafe too. we walked around for a little bit, and then he dropped me back home.

i met him two days later again. and then two days later again. i remember the time he said, i want you to be mine fully. i remember how he used to kneel down to tie my shoelaces without me asking. i remember when i got sick and he was so bothered by it. i remember when he said, what are we hiding from our parents? there's nothing wrong in being in love.

all of this happened in four months, by the way.

after i got sick, my mom started living with me because she was a little worried about my health, and i couldn't meet him that often. he got busy with his exams. and then the fault lines started to show. see, if you fight when you're apart, that's good. that's normal. we stopped talking. he was very busy with his exams, and i didn't want to bother him. i thought saying anything that might irritate him would be a distraction, and i didn't want to be that.

his last exam got over sometime in the middle of may the next year. he went back to his hometown for a wedding after that, and we couldn't meet. he came back, and we were supposed to meet that weekend. but i was getting irritated. why aren't we meeting? we live half an hour away. he sent me a huge paragraph saying he probably never loved me, and that he thinks both of us deserve something that is truly ours. i responded, okay.

i never met him after that."

"so you met for four months, mom, from august to december, then talked here and there, and then never met after that?"

"not never. just not for a really long time.

a year after that, i changed cities. i think all my friends thought i was running away from the idea of running into him. i probably was. after that, i moved to london.

a year later, i had gone to central london to meet one of my friends, and we were planning to grab some cocktail cans and grapes and cheese from the grocery store. i remember i was complaining to her about how much i hate pina colada and would much rather have a sauvignon blanc. and while i was saying this, standing in the middle of the wine section, i wasn't really looking at her. i saw a guy behind her who looked very familiar. he was at the billing counter. i couldn't see his face. his back was towards us.

my friend saw that i wasn't really looking at her. she turned around and said, loudly, who are you looking at?

and then someone shouted from behind us, hey, do you think we should get some beers as well?

he turned around. and our eyes locked.

it had been three years since we had seen each other. neither of us said anything. i was shocked. i looked behind him to see who he was with. it was his friend, who i knew used to live in london, at least back when i was in love with him.

he walked over to me and said, that's something i wasn't expecting to see in london. are you following me?

i said, in a very arrogant tone, no, i live here now.

i introduced my friend to him, and he introduced his friend to me. his friend said, i think we should give them a minute, and both of them left us in the middle of the wine section. i quickly swiped my eyes across all his fingers to see if there was anything on them. i think he was doing the same.

so, are you travelling? you finally made it to london, i said, sarcastically. he had always wanted to come to london. one of his exes used to live here. i don't even remember her name.

he looked at me and said, you seem to remember an awful lot about me.

i said, i don't know. when you start thinking, you start seeing a pattern. and sometimes you just can't forget the pattern.

hey. i'm sorry. i know it wasn't fair. it wasn't fair to you. i made you wait and then didn't even have the basic courtesy to say it to you in person. honestly, i knew i wouldn't be able to. how could i look at your face and say something like that?

i was trying to walk away. i didn't know how to respond to that.

hey, let's just grab lunch. maybe dinner. i'm here in london for another 10 days. and you live here. please, he said.

i would rather jump onto the tube tracks, i replied, and walked away.

he stood there, slightly smiling. i put the wine bottle back on the shelf, called out to my friend and left without buying anything. she turned around to look at him, shook her head and ran after me.

what happened? she asked.

he's sorry. he's sorry now.

and you didn't give in?

i guess i didn't, i said.

we walked away. we got back home that evening, and my brain was everywhere. what just happened? and how? i opened instagram, and there he was in my dms after three years, apologising. did he realise he fumbled? or does he just want to fuck around again? or did he just realise i'm much better without him, and he misses having me around? what is he even doing? why is he even here? my brain was everywhere.

but obviously, i did give in. i met him for lunch the next day. we started talking. he hadn't seen anybody in three years. he had tried to, it turned out, but it felt like he was cheating on me, he said. i hadn't seen anybody either. i told him i'd tried dating apps, but nothing ever seemed to work. i really couldn't delete you, i said. i think it was more the sauvignon blanc than me speaking at that point.

he asked me if i could take him around the city while his friend was at work. monday to friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., i'm all yours. take me around the way i took you around my city.

i caved.

ten days later, i remember we were standing in front of the london eye. he said, so this is it.

i said, i guess so.

so are you coming back to india? he asked.

i said, i'm graduating in two months, so yes. i don't think i'm going to get a job here. the economy sucks.

he said, okay. i'm looking forward to having you back.

having me back? you don't have me back.

he said, we'll see.

he hugged me. i hugged him back. he held me a little tighter and closed his eyes. i caved. i did it too. he kissed my forehead. goodbye, he said. see you, i replied. that coy smile again. he walked away.

we kept talking for three more months, until i was back. i got a job at one of my dream companies in india, in bangalore, the city where i first met him. we were back in the same city, more grown up. in the blink of an eye, we started dating again. it was more than it had ever been before. i met his mom and dad. he met mine. my mom was elated. she loved him and she loved his family. i remember she once told me that even she could not have found somebody like that. my dad and he were like best friends. they used to go out for long walks.

this went from first gear to fifth very quickly. and déjà vu, isn't it?

his mom started getting sick after that. he started getting irritated, impatient. he was in and out of hospitals more than he was home. he slept at the hospital more often than at home. he quit his job to help at home, which i thought was not the best idea. his dad was slipping into depression while his mom was slipping into death, and he was the only one who had to handle it all. we started to meet less often again. and instead of fighting about it, instead of talking about what was going on, we stopped again. it was again hi, how are you, is all okay? hi, i'm okay, all's fine.

his mom passed away. and he fell into a grief none of us were anticipating. he pushed me out of his life again. you deserve better than me. i need somebody who understands. i don't think we are meant to be with each other.

i was shattered. this was something i could never get over. he broke three hearts: mine and my parents'. i vowed to never let him in again. i moved to germany, and then france, and then the netherlands. i cut him out of my life completely. he tried to reach out, apologising, saying i did not mean any of that, saying i need you to be here with me. but i had vowed never to let him back in.

and then one day, a year later, i was really into baguettes, and i was in a bakery very close to my home. my cycle with the basket was parked outside, and i was inside the bakery. a shadow came from behind and stood next to me.

a baguette? it'll go bad. how much will you eat? you need to finish it in two days. it molds very quickly.

i looked up at him. there he was. i was in shock. i ran out of the shop, and he followed me. stop, please.

i turned around and screamed, you have no right to pounce into my life whenever you feel like it. who the hell do you think you are? why the hell are you here? what the hell? why are you trying to mess with me? every time, every time i'm trying to move away, and i am, i really am, why are you suddenly in front of me all the fucking time? why the hell are you here? what do you want from me? what more do you want from me? what more can i give you?

he said, no. no, i'm just here to apologise. i just want you back. i am so sorry. you see, i wasn't in the right place, and you know that. and that gives me no right to behave the way i did, but i'm a fucking child, and i did not know how to react to my fucking mother passing away. please, i want you back. we are meant to be. of course we are meant to be. however much we try to pull each other apart and drive each other away, somehow we're fucking back again. i didn't even know you were here. i had just come for an event at fucking tilburg university, and someone mentioned a girl here i might know. and it was you. why don't you understand? you and i are meant to be with each other. the universe wants us together. the world wants us together. however much we don't want it.

i looked at him. there was silence for 10 seconds.

say something. anything, he said.

there was a gust of wind, and my hair flew onto my face. i raised my left hand to push the strands away, and there it was: a flash of light off the ring i was wearing.

so you moved on, he said. you really did move on this time.

i took a step towards him. i took his left hand and cupped it in both of mine, and i said, it takes years to forget a moment, and a moment to forget years. i've forgotten you. if the universe wants us together, then the universe should know it doesn't dictate me anymore. i don't want to be loved like this. and hey, i loved you. i loved you in every way my heart could. i loved you even when you did not love me. and i will always love you. but i'm not in love with you anymore.

i pulled my hands back. and as my hands slipped away from his, those five seconds, those are what will be on loop in my last 60 seconds of life."
