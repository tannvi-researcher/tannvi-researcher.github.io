"in the end, what you remember most are the things you didn't do," says ivy. "i don't know when my clock is going to run out. but i can tell you exactly what i will remember in those last 60 seconds."

ivy is giving a lecture to the other women in the old age home.

"what do you think you will remember in your last 60 seconds?" asks ivy.

an old lady says, "i'll remember the day my son passed away, and i had to live knowing that what i created doesn't exist anymore."

another woman says, "the moment i'll have on loop in my last 60 seconds is the day my mother asked me to leave my husband because he was hitting me. i remember her exact words. she said, love never looks like that."

another woman asks, "ivy, what will you remember the most?"

ivy says out loud, "hey, i am the teacher. i am supposed to be asking the questions."

the class got over. everybody was giggling. ivy packed up her bag: just a tiffin box with some orange seeds, a book, her iphone and her wired earphones. her daughter had come to pick her up. she was volunteering as a counsellor at the old age home. not to forget, ivy herself was fairly old. it's just that she was a lot fitter, at least physically, than the other people in the home.

on the car ride back, ivy's daughter asks her, "so, how was it today? i don't understand why you still have to do all of this. you can just sit at home. i'm here for you, mom."

ivy looks at her and says, "i don't know. maybe this is what keeps me alive. knowing that i am still of use."

ivy's daughter just looks at her weirdly and continues driving. a while later she says, "you're very quiet today, mom. what's up? what was the class about? tell me about your day. what was your question of the day today?"

ivy always asked a question at the end of a session. something existential, absurd, weird or philosophical. just to leave behind some open thoughts, to keep the other women in the old age home wondering, thinking, to give them something interesting.

"i asked them what they will remember in the last 60 seconds of their life."

ivy's daughter says, "i assume you'll have a flashback of your whole life, childhood to old age, in 60 seconds. all the good memories, all the happy moments, picked out and put together like a short movie. that would be very satisfying, wouldn't it, mom?"

"i don't think i will have a flashback of my happy moments," ivy says. "i think i'll remember the exact moment when i held his hand for the last time."

"whose hand? grandad's?"

"no. there was a boy. i was 23 when i met him."

ivy remembers the first day she met him. "i hated coffee, you know?" she tells her daughter. he had asked her to come to one of those very artisanal cafes, the ones that are so particular about the kind of roast you want and stuff like that. ivy didn't care for any of that. she already knew this was not somebody she would particularly enjoy. she didn't like people who were too specific about their things.

"i know, i know, you'll say the amount of honey i want in my lemon tea in the morning says otherwise," ivy says. "but that 23-year-old girl isn't me anymore. that girl was just crazy. she had such high expectations from life."

ivy entered the cafe. she was wearing a pink shirt. he was wearing a checked white shirt with brown pants. she looked at him, they caught eyes, he stood up. she was half an hour late. she was wearing pjs, but with heels. she walked over to him and realised that even though she was wearing four-inch heels, he was fairly tall. that was a great tick on her checklist.

it was 6:30 on a thursday evening, the 16th of august, to be precise, when ivy met him. they talked till 12. in between, she got up twice. once to call her mom and lie to her that she was at home. and the second time because her senior had called, asking if she'd found anything in her research. she wasn't researching. she was with a boy. she was supposed to be researching, but she really liked that boy, and she didn't really care about the research anymore. she had just started working at that point, and she wasn't the biggest fan of the work she was doing, so she didn't care for it much.

they spoke about their jobs, their aspirations, growing up, childhood, parents, her sister. they spoke about marriage and finance. they were the last people in the cafe, and then the guy asked them to leave. he had already paid while ivy was in the washroom. she didn't get dinner that night. it was just some coffee and a piece of cake. he had made friends with the barista, even though it was his first time in that cafe too. they walked around for a little bit, and then he dropped her back home.

ivy met him two days later again. and then two days later again. she remembers the time he said, "i want you to be mine fully." she remembers how he used to kneel down to tie her shoelaces without her asking. she remembers when she got sick and he was so bothered by it. she remembers when he said, "what are we hiding from our parents? there's nothing wrong in being in love."

"all of this happened in four months, by the way," ivy says.

after ivy got sick, her mom started living with her because she was a little worried about her health, and ivy couldn't meet him that often. he got busy with his exams. and then the fault lines started to show. "see, if you fight when you're apart, that's good. that's normal," ivy says. they stopped talking. he was very busy with his exams, and ivy didn't want to bother him. she thought saying anything that might irritate him would be a distraction, and she didn't want to be that.

his last exam got over sometime in the middle of may the next year. he went back to his hometown for a wedding after that, and they couldn't meet. he came back, and they were supposed to meet that weekend. but ivy was getting irritated. why aren't we meeting? we live half an hour away. he sent her a huge paragraph saying he probably never loved her, and that he thinks both of them deserve something that is truly theirs. ivy responded, "okay."

"i never met him after that."

"so you met for four months, mom, from august to december, then talked here and there, and then never met after that?"

"not never. just not for a really long time."

a year after that, ivy changed cities. all her friends thought she was running away from the idea of running into him. she probably was. after that, she moved to london.

a year later, ivy had gone to central london to meet one of her friends, and they were planning to grab some cocktail cans and grapes and cheese from the grocery store. she was complaining to her friend about how much she hated pina colada and would much rather have a sauvignon blanc. and while she was saying this, standing in the middle of the wine section, she wasn't really looking at her friend. she saw a guy behind her who looked very familiar. he was at the billing counter. she couldn't see his face. his back was towards them.

her friend saw that ivy wasn't really looking at her. she turned around and said, loudly, "who are you looking at?"

and then someone shouted from behind them, "hey, do you think we should get some beers as well?"

he turned around. and their eyes locked.

it had been three years since they had seen each other. neither of them said anything. ivy was shocked. she looked behind him to see who he was with. it was his friend, who she knew used to live in london, at least back when she was in love with him.

he walked over to her and said, "that's something i wasn't expecting to see in london. are you following me?"

ivy said, in a very arrogant tone, "no, i live here now."

ivy introduced her friend to him, and he introduced his friend to her. his friend said, "i think we should give them a minute," and both of them left the two of them in the middle of the wine section. ivy quickly swiped her eyes across all his fingers to see if there was anything on them. he was doing the same.

"so, are you travelling? you finally made it to london," ivy said, sarcastically. he had always wanted to come to london. one of his exes used to live here. ivy didn't even remember her name.

he looked at her and said, "you seem to remember an awful lot about me."

ivy said, "i don't know. when you start thinking, you start seeing a pattern. and sometimes you just can't forget the pattern."

"hey. i'm sorry. i know it wasn't fair. it wasn't fair to you. i made you wait and then didn't even have the basic courtesy to say it to you in person. honestly, i knew i wouldn't be able to. how could i look at your face and say something like that?"

ivy was trying to walk away. she didn't know how to respond to that.

"hey, let's just grab lunch. maybe dinner. i'm here in london for another 10 days. and you live here. please," he said.

"i would rather jump onto the tube tracks," ivy replied, and walked away.

he stood there, slightly smiling. ivy put the wine bottle back on the shelf, called out to her friend and left without buying anything. her friend turned around to look at him, shook her head and ran after her.

"what happened?" she asked.

"he's sorry. he's sorry now."

"and you didn't give in?"

"i guess i didn't," ivy said.

they walked away. they got back home that evening, and ivy's brain was everywhere. what just happened? and how? she opened instagram, and there he was in her dms after three years, apologising. did he realise he fumbled? or does he just want to fuck around again? or did he just realise she's much better without him, and he misses having her around? what is he even doing? why is he even here? her brain was everywhere.

but obviously, ivy did give in. she met him for lunch the next day. they started talking. he hadn't seen anybody in three years. he had tried to, it turned out, but it felt like he was cheating on her, he said. ivy hadn't seen anybody either. she told him she'd tried dating apps, but nothing ever seemed to work. "i really couldn't delete you," ivy said. it was more the sauvignon blanc than ivy speaking at that point.

he asked her if she could take him around the city while his friend was at work. "monday to friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., i'm all yours. take me around the way i took you around my city."

ivy caved.

ten days later, they were standing in front of the london eye. he said, "so this is it."

ivy said, "i guess so."

"so are you coming back to india?" he asked.

ivy said, "i'm graduating in two months, so yes. i don't think i'm going to get a job here. the economy sucks."

he said, "okay. i'm looking forward to having you back."

"having me back? you don't have me back."

he said, "we'll see."

he hugged her. ivy hugged him back. he held her a little tighter and closed his eyes. ivy caved. she did it too. he kissed her forehead. "goodbye," he said. "see you," ivy replied. that coy smile again. he walked away.

they kept talking for three more months, until ivy was back. she got a job at one of her dream companies in india, in bangalore, the city where she first met him. they were back in the same city, more grown up. in the blink of an eye, they started dating again. it was more than it had ever been before. ivy met his mom and dad. he met hers. her mom was elated. she loved him and she loved his family. she once told ivy that even she could not have found somebody like that. ivy's dad and he were like best friends. they used to go out for long walks.

this went from first gear to fifth very quickly. "and déjà vu, isn't it?" ivy says.

his mom started getting sick after that. he started getting irritated, impatient. he was in and out of hospitals more than he was home. he slept at the hospital more often than at home. he quit his job to help at home, which ivy thought was not the best idea. his dad was slipping into depression while his mom was slipping into death, and he was the only one who had to handle it all. they started to meet less often again. and instead of fighting about it, instead of talking about what was going on, they stopped again. it was again "hi, how are you, is all okay?" "hi, i'm okay, all's fine."

his mom passed away. and he fell into a grief none of them were anticipating. he pushed ivy out of his life again. "you deserve better than me. i need somebody who understands. i don't think we are meant to be with each other."

ivy was shattered. this was something she could never get over. he broke three hearts: ivy's and her parents'. she vowed never to let him in again. she moved to germany, and then france, and then the netherlands. she cut him out of her life completely. he tried to reach out, apologising, saying "i did not mean any of that," saying "i need you to be here with me." but ivy had vowed never to let him back in.

and then one day, a year later, ivy was really into baguettes, and she was in a bakery very close to her home. her cycle with the basket was parked outside, and she was inside the bakery. a shadow came from behind and stood next to her.

"a baguette? it'll go bad. how much will you eat? you need to finish it in two days. it molds very quickly."

ivy looked up at him. there he was. she was in shock. she ran out of the shop, and he followed her. "stop, please."

ivy turned around and screamed, "you have no right to pounce into my life whenever you feel like it. who the hell do you think you are? why the hell are you here? what the hell? why are you trying to mess with me? every time, every time i'm trying to move away, and i am, i really am, why are you suddenly in front of me all the fucking time? why the hell are you here? what do you want from me? what more do you want from me? what more can i give you?"

he said, "no. no, i'm just here to apologise. i just want you back. i am so sorry. you see, i wasn't in the right place, and you know that. and that gives me no right to behave the way i did, but i'm a fucking child, and i did not know how to react to my fucking mother passing away. please, i want you back. we are meant to be. of course we are meant to be. however much we try to pull each other apart and drive each other away, somehow we're fucking back again. i didn't even know you were here. i had just come for an event at fucking tilburg university, and someone mentioned a girl here i might know. and it was you. why don't you understand? you and i are meant to be with each other. the universe wants us together. the world wants us together. however much we don't want it."

ivy looked at him. there was silence for 10 seconds.

"say something. anything," he said.

there was a gust of wind, and ivy's hair flew onto her face. she raised her left hand to push the strands away, and there it was: a flash of light off the ring she was wearing.

"so you moved on," he said. "you really did move on this time."

ivy took a step towards him. she took his left hand and cupped it in both of hers, and she said, "it takes years to forget a moment, and a moment to forget years. i've forgotten you. if the universe wants us together, then the universe should know it doesn't dictate me anymore. i don't want to be loved like this. and hey, i loved you. i loved you in every way my heart could. i loved you even when you did not love me. and i will always love you. but i'm not in love with you anymore."

ivy pulled her hands back. and as her hands slipped away from his, those five seconds, those are what will be on loop in her last 60 seconds of life.
