when i look back at where it all started, every day that i live today feels like a dream come true. but when i sit with myself, i feel like i'm living each day like a rebel. i have the freedom i wanted. i have the life that i wanted. the people, the friends, the career. i have been persistent, but i hate myself.

i haven't even touched my mid-twenties yet and i have a stable career. at least it looks like that. i have a schedule that someone younger than me would envy. i have a life planned out as promotions for the next five years. i have safety, a security net, exactly how the world defines success.

but this is not the girl who wanted to be a lawyer, who had pinned every picture of amal clooney on her pinterest in 10th standard. just because i wanted to dress up does not mean that i like corporate law. that girl must look at what i am right now and cry. maybe this is part of the dream, but this is not my dream. i wanted to be a lawyer because i liked the idea of being able to debate from both the sides. i wanted to be a lawyer because i thought that it has an immense amount of knowledge and information about society that no other career can ever give me.

i'm well spoken. i'm well read. i have a timetabled life. i have a predictable future. but this is not who i am. i'm living an ordinary dream. a dream i chose over risk, over uprooting my life once again. this life has come at the cost of health, personal relationships, mental health. this job lives inside my body more than i do. and then small things, like breathing slowly, removing the tension from your shoulders, unclenching your jaw, feel like the only bit of oxygen that i get in a day.

i'm living a compromised life. i'm postponing living and becoming. i remember that 15-year-old's first diary's first word was freedom. swadhinata. unafraid of who i am becoming. but i am so afraid of who i am. so afraid of finding out who i can become. i'm so afraid of figuring it all out. i think you need to lose all your sense of being to realise that what you are chasing is not worth chasing.

i feel like i'm living an unfinished life. i'm settling. i am shrinking. i'm trying to fit into relationships, life, job, community, friends, a social life that i think is stable. i have so many intentions that i think are just going away. but somehow i'm still stuck to that version of myself who would hate looking at me like this. and that feeling of being stuck is the most uncomfortable feeling. it makes me feel dead inside. but maybe this is what awakening really is. it's realising that you must grow past your pain. you must choose the unpredictable path, the path which will have burned bridges and empty, dark lanes. and i want to be able to step into that world with faith.

i think i'm living a settlement. i don't want my love to survive because i'm choosing it out of obligation, habit, fear of not finding something else out there. because in that world, leaving is fear. leaving is failure. it's not. i think my love should have the freedom to walk away. walk away when you feel small, quiet, or unsure. walk away when you feel like someone is putting you down. i want to build a life that invites love into it, not a life that contains it. i don't want to fit into someone else's largeness. and if demanding that is lonely, then maybe i'm choosing lonely for now.

i don't think 20s are meant to be lived with caution. they're meant to be lived with honesty, with an option to change, to reconfigure, to reroute, to lose faith in yourself and then regain it, to be unavailable to others because you want to be with yourself. i think i want to leave my 20s knowing that i chose growth, i chose courage. and i don't want to look back and mourn what i could have become. i want to stay restless. i want to listen to what i used to say.

i am coming for you, 20s.
