there's a quote from bojack horseman that goes something like: was it worth it to be happy for a little bit, even though it ended up sad? or would it have been better if the whole thing never happened?

sometimes i wonder, if i knew how it would end in the very beginning, would i allow myself to go through all of it again? and i think the unfortunate answer is yes, i would.

it's been one year. and it still burns like it was yesterday. sometimes i look back and i feel like i was living in a different dimension when i was with you. we acted as if we'd known each other all our lives, and we were pretending as if we were gonna be with each other for the rest of it as well. and it's funny when i think about it, that around the same time last year, i probably didn't even know who you were. maybe i still don't.

you know, there's a certain kind of people who come into your life to reconfigure you. they're not bad people. they're people who are very different from whatever you've seen so far. people whose eyes sparkle slightly differently from the rest of us. they don't announce when they come. they don't tell you who they are and why they've come. but before they leave, they rearrange something inside you, without asking your permission. and then they leave you with this new version of who you are, and you really can't put yourself back together after that. it's a new jigsaw.

and while it sounds beautiful when i describe it, it's the worst feeling in the world to look at yourself in the mirror and not find yourself ever again after that. it's like declaring a war over your own self. it's like you start burning. but you like it. you like the fact that they bothered you. it makes you feel like that bother perhaps is proof that it was real. and perhaps you measure what it was by what it has left you.

i think this kind of love is experienced in the aftermath, in the aftertaste. bitter or sweet, it left something behind, which you can't completely organise yourself around. and you start feeling indebted to them. and you finally understand what they said when they were leaving.

see, you also saw cracks. but then the cracks were not on you. they were on the person that they made. so you thought that they were needed for you to see yourself again. you start looking at yourself from their eyes. and it's beautiful. you find beauty in that version of you, until they leave. because then you are just left with the ruins. and the half-made plans, and the half-done dances, and the version of you who had built a life with them, but then there is no them in it anymore. and that person now cannot find the difference between the warmth of love and the burn of the fire it leaves you with.

and that's the real deal about this. it's different. it's heavy. it's like education. it's like educating yourself about who you truly are. and maybe that's not loss, in some ways. and that's the only honest definition i have for love right now.
