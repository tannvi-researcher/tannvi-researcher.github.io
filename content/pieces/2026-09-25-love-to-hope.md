the consequential price you pay of being in love is the hopes and wishes it leaves you with. these things don't leave you, long after love has. it reveals something deeper about who you are. sometimes you perform healing. and other days you use that same healing to project anger for self-development (maybe also, sometimes, self-sabotage). and sometimes, when you are honest with yourself, you realise that the number of times you search their name up on the internet to see what they're doing is indicative of where you stand.

what i'm going to say next is neither the performance of my healing, nor the projection of where i stand. i'm not trying to be the bigger person, or show that i've handled it with grace. i'm revealing what the consequential cost has shown me. which is that i want him to be happy.

i want him to reach wherever he wants to. i want to know that he's succeeding. i want him to feel that what he's doing is what he loves. i want his morning coffee to taste better. i want him to be able to finish a 5k under 30 minutes. i want him to find the love at home which he grew up seeing. i want his friends to call him on his birthday. i want him to travel to the places he always wished to. i want him to get a perfect score on all of his duolingo tests.

and most importantly, i want him to find the person who will love him precisely and exactly how he wants to be loved. and that is not how i came. that is not who i am. that was not me. which i've made peace with, somehow. i want him to feel that he's enough, and also realise that he always was, even though i could never make him feel like that.

and none of this is modesty or humility or generosity. it's the consequential price i paid of being in love. the idea that underneath all of those butterflies, and awareness, and burn, and the seeking of shelter, there's a wish and a hope that i have for a stranger in the crowd.

i think, in the most clear possible way, what remains with you after love is your inability to want anything less than the best for somebody else. and this time, this inability is not rooted in the want to be wanted back, or the fear of being left, or the ego of being the better person. it's just a very quiet sense of belief in the wellness of somebody else. what i've learned is that learning to love someone is letting them go and find that love in someone else, and be happy in the presence of somebody else. i can hold this hope and stand completely outside of his story. i can wish for his happiness without having any presence in it.

how can you love someone and then wish them anything less? you know you had something real when you are capable of wanting nothing more than someone else's success. and i mean each word of this. the consequential price you pay of being in love is not loss. it's the capability of wishing someone the best of luck in the world, and not being present in their story at all. that shows that you have it in you. and if you can love someone so much without getting anything in return, just imagine how it would be to receive this kind of love.

to be loved is to be blessed.
