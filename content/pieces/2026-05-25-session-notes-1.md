the one thing i get to hear the most from him these days is that i'm not being honest. and let's be honest, he's right.

i just tell him that i need space. i tell him that i just need you to push me a little bit more sometimes, and just give me space the other times. and the moment you see the colour on my face change, and the moment you see me fidgeting, you need to push even more. which is true, but not the whole truth again. a part of me really wants that to happen. a part of me would hate if that does.

i say it all as if i have figured myself out, as if i know how i am. i told him, the first session i ever met him, that i am a liar. i told him that i know myself enough, and at this very moment i'm not waiting to be asked if i know myself enough or not. but then he says, where did you go? and then my colour changed. because that's not me speaking, that's a different person speaking, because i wouldn't say that. if i knew myself, and if i knew i want to help myself, and i knew i want to change, and i want to modify, and i want to speak about those things, and i want to get it out of my body, then i wouldn't be here.

the truth is that it's way more smaller of an issue, and way more embarrassing. it's smaller in my head, i'd say, though. the full truth is that i need attention. and when the room slightly turns in a way that i don't want it to, i get very quiet and very, very closed very soon. and this all happens without me even consciously deciding to do so. it's happening without me consulting my own parts and my own brain and my own heart. it happens because i don't want to disappear, but that's my mechanism, i think. it's not that i want to be alone. it's that i want to be found.

and again, i know this about myself. and i have known this for a while. and i kept it in a part of myself which i don't give out in sessions. and that particular part just nods away and says, yeah, there is a background behind this. yeah, there's a story behind this. yeah, it's stemming from childhood. yeah, i see the pattern. i will work on it. yes, i will think about every time my body feels a little twitchy. yes, i will get better at describing it. yes, this makes me feel happy. and yes, this makes me feel sad. i don't know what this makes me feel anymore.

see, there's a child inside of me which, many, many, many years ago, learned that disappearing was a reliable way. it's when someone's looking at you and you just don't want to say plainly, please don't ignore me. the absence of somebody's attention is a great place to start for seeking that attention. the absence is when the door is not opening, so you just sit behind the door wanting someone to knock on it. the absence is when i don't want anyone to ask me where i went.

and i learned it. and i learned it in a very complicated way, in a very narcissistic, i don't know, self-consuming manner. and i still let myself do it, even now. i still let myself slip, even now. i let her do what she wants.

and you know what happens? this usually remains in my control, because i can see it. i can fathom what's happening inside my head and how i'm reacting to it. but then the moment someone says, oh, you're not good enough, oh, here it comes. the child comes back in a way that no one really asked for. and this kind of behaviour is for no one. that kind happens in my head, because sometimes the truth is more terrible than to say it out loud. and it is not something my body is intending to do. it is something i have learned to do. it's my instinct, it's my muscle.

i tell him that i have a really good relationship with criticism, and i can deal with it. i take criticism very well. that's a lie i've told to way too many people, way too many times. and it has started to feel like that's my personality now. i don't know. the lying? or the criticism not being taken well?

the truth is, i fold. i fold completely, without showing how, when, and where. and then i go somewhere else. somewhere i'm trying to get noticed. somewhere i'm no longer trying to be in the room, but waiting for someone to ask me to come.

and when no one does, i tell myself that that's the way i wanted it. that's the way i would prefer. i needed myself to be alone. i needed myself to give myself that space. i needed this independence, the solitude, and this is how i'm trying to become a better woman for myself.

trust me, she's lying. she's sitting by the door. she's sitting behind the door. so quietly, so quietly, so that she can listen to the first footstep that is approaching her.

i guess no one does.
