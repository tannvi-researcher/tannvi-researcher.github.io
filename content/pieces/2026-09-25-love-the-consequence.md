<p class="part-head"><span>to be known</span>22 september 2025</p>

he is the only one who knew the stories that i probably didn't tell anyone before that. i've always known that i acted weird around him from the very beginning. even before it all started, even before the possibility of it had even struck me. i used to pick fights on things which were not necessary as such. things which were the most stupid. but that was an opportunity for me to talk a little bit more with him, i guess. and he kind of got it as well, and would mirror my behaviour. i don't know, it was flirting. it was weird, it was flirting.

the most important feeling in this world that i think we as humans crave is the feeling to be seen, to be noticed, to be understood, to be known. this is nothing like the butterflies. it's not short-lived. it doesn't hit you like a storm. this happens slowly, with time. when someone notices when you are jittery or excited, or look brighter or duller on certain days. i think you stop looking at the faults in that particular person, because you know them too well. and you mistake being so aware of somebody for probably being in love. it's like a cognitive dissonance. you're not able to differentiate. the fault lines in them start getting blurry.

and the knowledge you have about them, as to which nerve to pick on, when to start chatting, when to be there, when to give space, that knowledge becomes the weapon. it's not even about the circumstances of how you were, and where you were, in that particular time of your life. it's about the person who defrauds you into believing that they know more about you than you yourself, perhaps. it's the general clarity about how much time and distance it will take, and how much of a soft voice and loud statements i should make, to make this person believe what i'm saying. it's the hot and cold.

and now that i've passed this, i think i always knew the answer under all of that. i knew the answer of why it shaped up the way it did. and this left a kind of mark on me, which i don't think anybody can love out of me now.

and you know, when it's two in the night and you look back, and you think to yourself that i gave you the wound. i gave you the map to the wound, and the antidote. i told you where it'll hurt me, and told you how to help me with it. and you chose to do the very opposite. i told you what i hate the most about relationships. i told you what i hate the most about love. and you did exactly that. you waited until i hated that you knew me. you waited until i despised i ever liked you. as if i despised i ever gave you that key. i ever gave you that map. so yes, to be known is to be hurt.

and that's why i think that you should never date a friend. they know too much.

<p class="part-head"><span>to burn like fire</span>24 january 2026</p>

there's a quote from bojack horseman that goes something like: was it worth it to be happy for a little bit, even though it ended up sad? or would it have been better if the whole thing never happened?

sometimes i wonder, if i knew how it would end in the very beginning, would i allow myself to go through all of it again? and i think the unfortunate answer is yes, i would.

it's been one year. and it still burns like it was yesterday. sometimes i look back and i feel like i was living in a different dimension when i was with you. we acted as if we'd known each other all our lives, and we were pretending as if we were gonna be with each other for the rest of it as well. and it's funny when i think about it, that around the same time last year, i probably didn't even know who you were. maybe i still don't.

you know, there's a certain kind of people who come into your life to reconfigure you. they're not bad people. they're people who are very different from whatever you've seen so far. people whose eyes sparkle slightly differently from the rest of us. they don't announce when they come. they don't tell you who they are and why they've come. but before they leave, they rearrange something inside you, without asking your permission. and then they leave you with this new version of who you are, and you really can't put yourself back together after that. it's a new jigsaw.

and while it sounds beautiful when i describe it, it's the worst feeling in the world to look at yourself in the mirror and not find yourself ever again after that. it's like declaring a war over your own self. it's like you start burning. but you like it. you like the fact that they bothered you. it makes you feel like that bother perhaps is proof that it was real. and perhaps you measure what it was by what it has left you.

i think this kind of love is experienced in the aftermath, in the aftertaste. bitter or sweet, it left something behind, which you can't completely organise yourself around. and you start feeling indebted to them. and you finally understand what they said when they were leaving.

see, you also saw cracks. but then the cracks were not on you. they were on the person that they made. so you thought that they were needed for you to see yourself again. you start looking at yourself from their eyes. and it's beautiful. you find beauty in that version of you, until they leave. because then you are just left with the ruins. and the half-made plans, and the half-done dances, and the version of you who had built a life with them, but then there is no them in it anymore. and that person now cannot find the difference between the warmth of love and the burn of the fire it leaves you with.

and that's the real deal about this. it's different. it's heavy. it's like education. it's like educating yourself about who you truly are. and maybe that's not loss, in some ways. and that's the only honest definition i have for love right now.

<p class="part-head"><span>to seek shelter</span>25 may 2026</p>

as a child, every book you read, every movie you watch, every song you listen to, describes that we as humans have come to find our other half. your goal. the person you go back to with all of your vulnerability, honesty and love. that one person you love without having any conditions in return. and that love without conditions is the love you're told you'll find in your partner. they tell you that that person will be the only place you'll probably be honest with. the only thing you'll probably be honest with.

i grew up as a hopeless romantic. i was willing to put my walls down, and find my shelter in someone else, to put my vulnerability down and give them my honesty. i was willing to feel the butterflies, to be known, and to feel the fire burn through me. i was waiting to be cracked open, to be read like a book.

and every time i reached somewhere close to that, it kept on asking for more. more for me to be okay with. more for me to swallow. to blur the line, to not realise who you've become in the process of loving somebody else. and somehow i was told that it's beautiful. we still write songs about these things. they call it the ache of being wanted, the suffering in order to obtain somebody. the loosening of self.

and if that's the honest explanation of how it's supposed to feel, if this is how love is, then i think we have just renamed and romanticised loneliness. we have dressed it up in a way that looks pretty and not desperate. how can one's willingness to ruin themselves be good for anyone? this cannot be noble. this cannot be why and how i'm supposed to be. this is not me.

we are building altars to things we know will light us up in fire. i think the hunger of being wanted is what has been called love. you needing to keep your house's doors cracked open, with the hope that you will see someone's face the next morning, is just loneliness which kills you on a daily basis. not love. you feeding yourself this expectation at 3 a.m. after you've put down something heavy from your life, there's no relief.

i think the cruelest part of all of this is that you cannot even rage about it. underneath all of these lies, things are unmoved. even if you are trying to be blunt and honest about the version of love that has been given to you like an heirloom, and told to you like mythology, you will still crack that door open. because you are waiting.

so perhaps love is loneliness.

<p class="part-head"><span>to hope</span>25 september 2026</p>

the consequential price you pay of being in love is the hopes and wishes it leaves you with. these things don't leave you, long after love has. it reveals something deeper about who you are. sometimes you perform healing. and other days you use that same healing to project anger for self-development (maybe also, sometimes, self-sabotage). and sometimes, when you are honest with yourself, you realise that the number of times you search their name up on the internet to see what they're doing is indicative of where you stand.

what i'm going to say next is neither the performance of my healing, nor the projection of where i stand. i'm not trying to be the bigger person, or show that i've handled it with grace. i'm revealing what the consequential cost has shown me. which is that i want him to be happy.

i want him to reach wherever he wants to. i want to know that he's succeeding. i want him to feel that what he's doing is what he loves. i want his morning coffee to taste better. i want him to be able to finish a 5k under 30 minutes. i want him to find the love at home which he grew up seeing. i want his friends to call him on his birthday. i want him to travel to the places he always wished to. i want him to get a perfect score on all of his duolingo tests.

and most importantly, i want him to find the person who will love him precisely and exactly how he wants to be loved. and that is not how i came. that is not who i am. that was not me. which i've made peace with, somehow. i want him to feel that he's enough, and also realise that he always was, even though i could never make him feel like that.

and none of this is modesty or humility or generosity. it's the consequential price i paid of being in love. the idea that underneath all of those butterflies, and awareness, and burn, and the seeking of shelter, there's a wish and a hope that i have for a stranger in the crowd.

i think, in the most clear possible way, what remains with you after love is your inability to want anything less than the best for somebody else. and this time, this inability is not rooted in the want to be wanted back, or the fear of being left, or the ego of being the better person. it's just a very quiet sense of belief in the wellness of somebody else. what i've learned is that learning to love someone is letting them go and find that love in someone else, and be happy in the presence of somebody else. i can hold this hope and stand completely outside of his story. i can wish for his happiness without having any presence in it.

how can you love someone and then wish them anything less? you know you had something real when you are capable of wanting nothing more than someone else's success. and i mean each word of this. the consequential price you pay of being in love is not loss. it's the capability of wishing someone the best of luck in the world, and not being present in their story at all. that shows that you have it in you. and if you can love someone so much without getting anything in return, just imagine how it would be to receive this kind of love.

to be loved is to be blessed.
