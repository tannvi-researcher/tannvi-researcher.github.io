**Hi, I’m Tannvi!** I’m a policy analyst at the [Centre for Responsible AI, IIT Madras](https://cerai.iitm.ac.in/), where I work on responsible AI, privacy and data governance, and on how new technology meets people and institutions. I started out in [law](/cv/) and found my way into research and policy.

When I’m not doing that, I write [poems and short stories](/scratch/), [reread Camus](/soapbox/the-cost-of-a-story/), and keep a [list of things to do before I die](/before-i-die/).

## How I got here

Here’s how I got to where I am…

I have a law degree and I still click “I agree” without reading. It stopped bothering me when I saw that it’s by design. Apps make the “yes, cancel” button red because people avoid red buttons. Design already knows consent is steered. Law still calls the click a choice. Most of what I do comes back to that gap.

I began doubting that choice at 14, when a boy at school threatened to post a morphed picture of me, my face on someone else’s body. Printed copies had already reached the boys’ washroom. I kept his letter and took it to the principal. He got a warning and the pictures came down. I still don’t know if every copy did. It was crude and would fool no one. Today, a convincing one takes a phone and a few seconds. That picture is part of why I chose law.

I studied law at [CHRIST University](https://christuniversity.in/departments/main-campus/school-of-law/school-of-law) in Bengaluru from 2019 to 2024, and somewhere along the way I got pulled towards technology. I wrote and edited for [The Cyber Blog India](https://cyberblogindia.in/), did an advanced diploma in [cyber security and data protection law](https://nalsarpro.softpal.in/Study-Here/One-Year-Advanced-Diploma-Programme-on-offer/Cyber-Security-Data-Protection-Laws/About-the-Programme) at NALSAR, took [Harvard’s CS50](https://harvardonline.harvard.edu/course/cs50-introduction-computer-science) to understand the technology I kept talking about, and co-founded the [Law and Technology Committee](https://ltcslcu.com/) at college. In law school I also spent months with a small team that got non-consensual images taken down online. Our senior told us never to promise a client full removal. The law could offer something partial, and only after the damage. In between, I spent an unreasonable amount of law school on moots, the [Vis](https://www.vismoot.org/) in Vienna and the [Jessup](https://www.ilsa.org/jessup/), and wrote my [dissertation](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7691703) on the personalisation of internet consumers’ data.

At [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/) I worked on the opposite problem: making rules easier to follow. Clients couldn’t answer the questionnaires for India’s data protection law, so I redesigned them, and turnaround fell from six months to one or two. But a rule people can follow isn’t always a rule that’s right, and law gave me no way to tell which one I was building. Along the way I wrote op-eds for [Mint](https://www.livemint.com/opinion/online-views/ani-vs-openai-delhi-high-court-ruling-intellectual-property-ai-training-copyright-11785477510864.html), [CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/legal/dpdpa-personal-data-protection-india-new-law-compliance-gccs-multinational-companies-views-19914155.htm) and [NDTV Profit](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/india/admission-enquiry-to-everyday-classroom-data-impact-of-indias-new-data-protection-law-on-the-education-sector-11135692).

So I went outside law. Since August 2026 I’ve been at [CeRAI](https://cerai.iitm.ac.in/), and I also research at the [Center for Democratic AI](https://centerfordemocraticai.org/). Much of that work is on bystander privacy: the person on the pavement who walks past someone in smart glasses and never sees a notice. The studies I’ve reviewed found little evidence that notice changes whether people accept being recorded. Purpose and recipient seem to matter more. I keep landing on Helen Nissenbaum’s [contextual integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contextual_integrity), the idea that information should flow the way a setting leads people to expect.

Camus, one of my favourite writers, says integrity has no need of rules. I want to agree with him, and I can’t. Rules exist for the person who must be protected from someone else’s idea of right. What I want to understand is what a rule must rest on to deserve being followed. I wrote about where that leads in [Everyone Is a Bystander Now](/soapbox/everyone-is-a-bystander-now/).

On the side, I co-chair media at the [Moot Alumni Association](https://maa.net/core-team/) and arbitrate the same Vis Moot I once competed in. The full list lives on my [CV](/cv/).
